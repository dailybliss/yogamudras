Apan Mudra

also known as Apana Mudra · the seal of elimination

The gesture of cleansing and release. Joining the middle and ring fingers to the thumb, Apan Mudra works on the body's downward-flowing energy to support digestion, elimination and natural detox.

  • Best forDigestion, detox, release

What Apan Mudra means

Apana Vayu is one of the five vital winds, or vayus, of the body — a current of prana whose main work is elimination. It governs everything between the navel and the perineum: the small intestine, the colon, and the urinary and reproductive systems.

When this energy flows freely, the body releases what it no longer needs — stools, gas and urine — with ease, and it supports menstruation and full-term childbirth too. Apana naturally moves downward, and when it is disturbed the result can be constipation, diarrhoea, piles or menstrual trouble. Apan Mudra is the gesture used to bring this downward current back into balance.

▽

The downward current

Where some mudras lift energy upward, Apan Mudra works with the body's downward flow — the direction of release and letting go. It is a gesture as much about clearing the mind of heavy emotion as clearing the body.

How to practise Apan Mudra

  1. Take a steady seatSit with a straight spine in any meditation posture you like — Sukhasana, Padmasana, Siddhasana, Swastikasana or Vajrasana.
  2. Rest your handsPlace your hands on your thighs or knees with the palms facing upward.
  3. Settle the breathClose your eyes and take a few slow, relaxed breaths, letting your attention rest on the breathing itself. You can keep your eyes open, but closing them helps you focus.
  4. Form the gestureOn both hands at once, bend your middle and ring fingers in to meet the tip of your thumb. Keep your index and little fingers pointing straight out.
  5. Hold and visualiseStay here for 15 to 30 minutes, or as long as is comfortable, picturing the flow of energy moving through your body.

Tip: keep the index and little fingers relaxed and extended rather than stiff. Practise with both hands together for the full effect.

Benefits of Apan Mudra

Digestion and elimination

Supports the health of the small and large intestines, relieves constipation, and eases indigestion and acidity.

Natural detox

Helps the body clear physical waste and toxins — the reason it is so closely linked with cleansing and detoxification.

Urinary and pelvic health

Relieves difficulty passing urine, strengthens the organs of the pelvic region, and helps regulate the menstrual cycle.

Emotional release

Traditionally used to help you feel grounded, process difficult emotions and let go of what weighs on the mind.

Heart, chest and back

Valued for easing back pain and chest discomfort, and considered supportive for those with respiratory or heart complaints.

Energy and glow

Said to kindle energy in the liver and gallbladder, strengthen the thighs and hips, and bring a healthy glow to the skin.

How long and how often

15–30minutes a session
Bothhands together
Dailyor as needed

Hold Apan Mudra for 15 to 30 minutes at a time, or for as long as it stays comfortable, with both hands at once.

It can be practised daily as part of your routine, or turned to whenever you need help with digestion, elimination or a sense of release.

Practising with care

  • Let the fingers rest gently against the thumb — there is no need to press hard. Release if you feel any strain.
  • Choose a seat you can hold comfortably for the full session; a cushion or chair is fine if the floor postures are difficult.
  • Mudras complement a healthy lifestyle and medical care — they are not a replacement for it. For ongoing digestive, urinary or heart concerns, read the practice guidelines and speak to your doctor.

Apan Mudra — common questions

What is Apan Mudra good for?
It mainly supports the body's downward-flowing energy — helping digestion and elimination, relieving constipation, easing acidity, and supporting the body's natural detox.
Which fingers are used in Apan Mudra?
The middle and ring fingers bend in to touch the tip of the thumb, while the index and little fingers stay extended. It is done with both hands.
How long should I hold Apan Mudra?
Hold it for 15 to 30 minutes at a time, or for as long as it remains comfortable.
Is Apan Mudra the same as Apan Vayu Mudra?
No. They share a name but are different gestures. Apan Mudra uses the middle and ring fingers with the thumb; Apan Vayu Mudra folds the index finger in as well and is used more for the heart.
Can Apan Mudra help with menstrual problems?
Yes. By balancing the apana current that governs the pelvic organs, it is traditionally used to help regulate the menstrual cycle.
